# Smart Farms RWA Platform White Paper

## Smart Farms RWA Platform: Commodity Based Yield Generating RWA Platform

Abstract. A platform for creating digital tokens leveraging yield-generating real-world assets (RWA) from agroforestry. sfUSD will be the first token. Having a dynamic annual yield (starting at 15% APY) based on a broad set of RWAs, topped-up monthly, available to both individuals and organizations. sfUSD is a decentralized method of creating and exchanging value on the blockchain using precision agriculture and forestry methods to create the yield. sfUSD issuers and market participants take advantage of blockchain technology, to access liquidity and build real world value that can be represented in the token and returned as yield to the holders.  To maintain transparency & long-term stability of the yield, we propose methods of performance certification, and proof of payment accounts, to demonstrate the value of the sfUSD token. By proving the connection between the RWA and the number of issued tokens sfUSD can build reputation in the market and demonstrate source of yield over time. Built on well-established structures and systems, sfUSD uses the ERC20 permissionless smart contract protocol. And RWA verification redundancies require both drone and Google Earth topographical surveillance audits – with precise GPS coordinates of agricultural and forestry assets - to ensure the authenticity of the RWAs providing yield to the sfUSD platform.

## Introduction

Blockchain technology, along with cryptocurrency, is now part of the mainstream of global finance accounting for trillions of dollars in value and tens of trillions in annual transactions. Tools and innovations are widely used across the world, from Nigeria to Norway, with adoption of smart contracts, using the blockchain for record keeping & automation, growing exponentially. This neutral global platform for payments and assets makes the future of commerce and industry a massive opportunity to place the tools of value creation, value storage, and value transfer in the hands of billions of people.

While tremendous digital value is being created, and tools propagated, there is a growing sense in the marketplace that the physical world is underrepresented by the technological platforms, and that real world assets (RWA) represented on the blockchain will greatly enhance the utility, both perceived and actual, of creating, storing, and leveraging value.

## Current Market for RWA Tokenization

The vast majority of the world’s physical and tradeable assets – land, commodities, stocks and bonds - are not tokenized on the blockchain. Everything could be and likely will be. But not yet. Why?

Until about 2023, the technology just wasn’t ready for RWA tokenization. However, with the recent launch of Layer 2s such as Base on the Ethereum ecosystem, transactions are now cheaper ($0.01 each) & faster (200 Milliseconds). In addition, with the maturing of stablecoins and smart contract primitives being battle tested in the real world with hundreds of billions of dollars, the technology now exists to build the sfUSD platform.

In terms of the overall market, tokenization has not occurred in developed economies because assets are already well-defined in formal legal terms and are mostly fungible. And with formal economic structures and governance, there is little urgency to tokenize these assets as they are already leveraged and monetized via well-established legal and contractual methods. These assets in developed economies, thus defined, represent the vast majority of securitized collateral transactions and global GDP. There is sufficient scarcity of these mature assets, with little need to tokenize as RWA for digital transactions. Fiduciary access to these assets for placement on digital platforms is limited, and expensive, since there are other ways to leverage the value. The competitive advantage for mature RWAs, with or without tokenization, belongs to large institutional investors, strategic beneficial owners and governments.

In frontier markets, the opposite is true. Assets are not well-defined in legal terms and are therefore non-fungible. Emerging markets real world assets represent the greatest untapped source of tokenization and placement in the digital marketplace. The current and future value of these assets, in Africa and other frontier markets, represents the vast majority of untapped raw materials and arable land in the world. A significant percentage of minerals, oil and gas, land, and fresh water, available for development in the future exists in these markets.

## Technology Stack and Processes

Every sfUSD released into circulation will be connected to equivalent real-world agroforestry and yield-generating assets. The yield will be dynamic, however initially it will target a 15% APY.   Each project creating their RWA is individually responsible for maintaining their real world assets and paying out yield to the holders of sfUSD.  For example as a maintainer of RWAs, Small Farm Cities will act as a party responsible for conducting the audits and making all the materials public. The risk is mitigated through performance certification, and proof of payment reserve accounts, reinforcing the value of the sfUSD. Built on well-established structures and systems, sfUSD utilizes the ERC20 permissionless smart contract protocol. RWA verification redundancies include both drone and Google Earth topographical surveillance audits with precise GPS coordinates of agricultural and forestry assets, ensuring the transparency of the sfUSD platform.

## sfUSD Technology Stack

The stack comprises multiple layers and possesses several features that are best comprehended through a diagram.

In the first layer, the Base 2nd layer on the Ethereum blockchain hosts the sfUSD token, created with the ERC-20 standard. ERC-20 is a standard that defines how smart contracts can manage fungible tokens on Ethereum. It ensures all ERC-20 tokens are interoperable and compatible within the ecosystem. 

Ethereum uses its own consensus mechanism to secure and maintain the integrity of the blockchain and its transactions.

The second layer is the smart contract proof of reserve payments account, which will hold three months of yield payments. And the second layer will include the rebasing payment function, which handles the automatic redistribution of yield via USDC tokens among holders of sfUSD to maintain the target yield.

## Flow of Funds Process

The sfUSD lifecycle includes seven steps, best illustrated with a diagram.
  
Example of an RWA Creator

Step 1 – Small farm Cities Africa (SFC), a Delaware-registered company, receives funds into U.S. bank account.

Step 2 – SFC creates RWA agroforestry yield-generating assets, along with monitoring all quality assurance, and performance elements.

Step 3 – sfUSD tokens are created and sent to the address of the USD provider. This is how all sfUSD tokens enter circulation. Amount of funds received, deposited by the user, and denominated in USD fiat currency, equals the corresponding number of sfUSD on a 1:1 basis and is issued to the user.

Step 4 – sfUSD tokens can be added to Uniswap or Aerodrome systems. Uniswap is a decentralized exchange (DEX) on Ethereum for trading cryptocurrencies without intermediaries. Aerodrome, a MetaDEX on Base, combines features of Curve, Convex, and Uniswap to act as the main trading and liquidity hub for the Base ecosystem.

Step 5 – Small Farm Cities RWA fiat payments reflecting dynamic yield (targeting ~15%) are deposited in a cryptocurrency exchange and financial technology company (example currently used by Small Farm Cities: Yellowcard Financial LLC) providing a platform for buying, selling, and storing digital currencies, particularly in emerging markets across Africa. Companies will have robust security measures in place such as multi-sig wallets, authenticators, and other procedures to ensure the security of the token issuance process. 

Step 6 – Yield is paid to sfUSD token holders and deposited as USDC in user digital wallets

## Proof of Payments

sfUSD will establish a Proof of Payments (PoP) process to demonstrate 15% yield coverage for sfUSD holders for 90 days, allowing the platform to demonstrate that it holds sufficient assets to distribute yield, building trust and transparency by verifying the validity of transactions.

As a further measure of PoP assurance, sfUSD will provide users with routine asset verification redundancies with both drone and Google Earth topographical surveillance audits – with precise GPS coordinates of agricultural and forestry assets - to ensure the authenticity of the yield-generating RWA value on the sfUSD platform.

## Implementation Weaknesses/Risk Mitigation Measures

The implementation of sfUSD does not create a guaranteed RWA yield-generating token. The yield will be dynamic and fluctuate over time based on the real world performance of the agroforestry assets. 

The following weaknesses represent risks to sfUSD users that the platform must address and counter with effective mitigation measures:

- Devaluation of assets 
- Regulatory risks 
- Liquidity risks
- Yield sustainability

Devaluation of assets. sfUSD RWAs will leverage African rural markets with plentiful affordable, arable land with historically consistent climatic conditions. While extreme weather could cause seasonal downward yield pressure upon agriculture and forestry assets, irrigation systems will be standard infrastructure for sfUSD RWAs, along with best practices in soil fertility conservation and erosion prevention. Properly managed agricultural land and forests will hold value in perpetuity.

Regulatory risks. sfUSD will be deployed and managed by Small Farm Cities Africa, an experienced, qualified, registered project developer that will secure rights to assets with the appropriate operating licenses in compliance with all relevant national and local laws and ordinances.  sfUSD RWAs will meet and exceed national and international environmental and social impact standards. The assets will also be accessible to local and national partners, ensuring domestic ownership.

Liquidity risks. sfUSD RWAs will generate high value, high demand commodities in the affordable and sustainable products to achieve consistent yields. There are local, regional, and export markets for raw and processed agricultural and forestry products.

Yield sustainability. The target of 15% annual yield balances conservative assumptions on the cost of land, labor rates, and yields. With proper transparency, agricultural and forestry products will meet food security and affordable housing goals for a rapidly growing population for the foreseeable future.

Access to foreign stock exchanges and securities for deployment of national reserves comes with costs and inconveniences, and in any case do nothing to help stabilize national currencies or build local infrastructure with local resources.
